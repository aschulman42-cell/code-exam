# The drafter's ceiling on real claims

*Status as of 2026-10-23. Every number on this page carries the date it was measured and the
number of runs behind it. The table is regenerated by
`scripts/recap-chart-drafter-ceiling.mjs --render`; the prose is revised by hand.*

## The question

Given the very functions one party says practice the claim, does CodeExam's pseudo-claim
drafter write a claim that reads like the asserted one? The agreement harness
([`claim-chart-agreement.md`](claim-chart-agreement.md)) measures whether `--claim-locate`
finds the code a litigant cited; this page measures the other half of the distance. If the
drafter, seeded with MARA's cited functions and nothing else, does not write the order-q test,
then the drafter is the limit and the locator cannot be blamed for not finding it. If it does,
the distance between the patent's language and the code's mechanism is one the locator has to
cross on its own.

Said plainly, as the rest of the claim-chart pages say it: this is agreement with a party's
theory, not correctness. Any litigant can be wrong, so a drafter that writes the asserted claim
from the litigant's code has reproduced that party's reading, not confirmed it.

## The method

Per filing and repository, from the harness's work directory:

1. **Anchors.** The litigant's cited functions that the index holds, resolved to `file@symbol`
   (exact name first, then the last segment; where several files define the name, the chart's
   cited files first, then at most three per name), in chart order, capped at 24. Nothing from
   the patent reaches the drafter: no claim text, number, class or topic.
2. **Draft.** `--pseudo-claims @anchors.lst --claim-schema --claim-class both --claims-only`,
   so the seed yields a method claim and a system claim with their sidecar. A **floor** draft from
   a group of the same size taken from the grouper's own candidates
   (`--pseudo-claims --candidates`), the first group sharing no function with the litigant's set.
3. **Compare.** One model call per pair, element by element: the asserted claim's elements are
   Google Patents' divisions, classed as preamble, mechanism or generic; the judge returns MET,
   PARTIAL or ABSENT per element with the pseudo-claim's phrase quoted, and the pseudo-claim's
   elements with no counterpart. Three pairs per filing: the litigant-seeded claim against the
   asserted claim (**the measure**), the floor-seeded claim against the asserted claim (**the
   floor**), and the litigant-seeded claim against another filing's claim of the same art (**the
   control**).
4. **Score.** Mechanism elements met or partial over mechanism elements is the headline, with
   a strict count (MET alone) beside it; all elements and the reverse count follow. A measure is
   reported as a finding only where it exceeds both the floor and the control for that filing, on
   each read separately; otherwise the row says indistinguishable, which is itself the finding: a
   claim so generic any mechanism meets it, or a comparison the judge cannot make. The strict
   read exists because the first run showed PARTIAL to be lenient: the judge gave four PARTIALs
   to another signing claim of the same art on shared words such as "modular reduction".

## Reading the rows

The first three (2026-10-23, Claude Sonnet 4.6 as drafter and judge, one sample per row), read
as the questions the page opened with:

**MARA 1-11 (US 7,372,961 against Bitcoin Core): does the drafter write the order-q test?** It
writes the loop and the check as the code states them, not as the claim does. Seeded with the
eight functions the chart cites (`CKey::MakeNewKey`, `CKey::Check`, `secp256k1_ec_seckey_verify`,
`GetStrongRandBytes`, `MakeKeyData` among them), the method claim reads: "verifying the private
key candidate by loading the candidate bytes as a scalar on the secp256k1 elliptic curve and
confirming the scalar is within the valid range; repeating the generating and verifying steps
until the private key candidate passes verification". The judge marks the repeat MET and the
determination, acceptance and rejection PARTIAL ("checks validity but does not state this is a
less-than-q comparison performed prior to any mod-q reduction"). The hash step is PARTIAL for a
different reason: the draft's hash is the RNG's internal SHA-512 accumulator, and "B does not
perform a distinct hash H() on a seed value SV to produce H(SV)". The floor draft (an unrelated
Bitcoin Core group) meets nothing; the control (the same draft against ViaBTC's claim) meets
nothing. A finding on both reads, 6 of 6 mechanism elements met or partial, 1 MET. Read with the
chart of 2026-10-22, which found the same code's hash step absent and its range check assumed
behind an opaque callee: the distance on MARA's hash element is in the code, not in the locator
and not in the drafter. The drafter's ceiling here is the mechanism in the code's terms; the
claim's H(SV) is the claim's.

**Foundry 1-11 (US 8,806,197 against secp256k1): does the drafter write the recovery indicator?**
Yes: "computing a recovery identifier from the parity of the y-coordinate of the ephemeral point
and an overflow flag", MET, from `secp256k1_ecdsa_sig_sign` and the fourteen functions beside it.
The signature-component element is PARTIAL (the draft's r and s against the claim's first and
second components with an ephemeral public-key coordinate). The floor meets nothing. The control,
the same draft against Foundry 1-14's finite-field claim, is PARTIAL on four of six elements on
shared words ("modular reduction", "ephemeral point"), which is why the headline reads
indistinguishable from the control while the strict read, MET alone, is a finding (1 against 0).
That control row is the page's own evidence that PARTIAL is lenient and that the strict column
belongs beside the headline.

**Securitize 15-9 (US 12,099,996 against dstoken): does the drafter write the remote signing?**
No, and the row's numbers understate how clearly. The asserted claim is 25 elements, a system
claim whose processor, memory and interface boilerplate the classifier calls generic, along
with most of the signing and verification sub-elements, so the headline counts five mechanism
elements and reads 2 of 5 met or partial (the whitelist restriction and a verification
sub-element, both PARTIAL) against a floor of 1 of 5 and a control of 1 of 6; a finding on the
headline, indistinguishable from the floor on the strict read, with no MET anywhere. Read the
elements, not the counts: every element that involves the remotely located device
cryptographically signing data, communicating it, and the system verifying that the target
address derives from the private key that signed (A3, A10, A12, A14 to A18, A21, A24) is ABSENT.
The draft, seeded with `transfer`, `transferFrom`, `recordTransfer` and six more, writes
compliance gates, a token ledger and investor-count recording, which is what those functions
do. The cited code does no signing; the claim's signer is another machine. The chart judge of
2026-10-22 read the same claim's signing onto the permit path; the drafter, given only the
litigant's functions, does not reach it at all. Agreement with the litigant's citation, here,
would have been the error.

**Across the three.** The drafter reaches what the cited code does and writes it in the code's
terms; it does not write what the claim asserts beyond the code (MARA's H(SV), Securitize's
remote signer), and a judge that counts PARTIAL will credit shared vocabulary across claims of
one art. The reverse lists (B-only: 13, 9, 8 elements) are the code's specifics the claims do not
recite: nonce iteration and blinded multiplication for Foundry, secure-memory handling for MARA,
investor-count recording for Securitize.

**Across the fourteen** (the table above, 2026-10-23, the same engine, one sample per row).
Fifteen pairs scored; VM Innovations' circlefin and go-ethereum repositories have no anchor the
index resolves (the chart's cited names there are a local's `wait` four ways) and stay unscored.
On the headline read, eleven of fifteen measures exceed both their floor and their control. On the
strict read, three: ViaBTC (2 MET of 2), MARA and Foundry 1-11 (1 each); on the other twelve the
judge never says MET, only PARTIAL, which is the granularity gap of the page's question stated
as a number: given the litigant's own functions, the drafter writes the mechanism at the code's
grain, and the claim's element is met in part. Two measures are zero from the litigant's own
anchors, Databricks (0 of 3) and PROGME (0 of 4), the two whose anchor lists were capped at 24
(from 44 and 30) in chart order, so the cut may have taken the crux; a re-run with the cap lifted
is the obvious next measurement. One floor beats its measure, Core Scientific 1-8 (floor 4 of 5
against 3 of 5): a claim an unrelated secp256k1 group meets as well as the cited one, which is
what the floor exists to say. True Return has no floor: dss's 262 symbols yield no candidate
group disjoint from the cited set, so its row is read against the control alone. Every
comparison parsed on the first call. Cost: 29 drafts at $2.81 and 52 comparisons at $0.53, $3.34
for the fourteen. The floor's deterministic grouper (`--pseudo-claims --candidates`) took 52 minutes on
Spark's 93,776 symbols and 91 on xbmc's 57,568, a cost of its own worth an issue.

## Caveats

- Agreement with a party, not correctness.
- The drafter cannot run blind: it reads the code and its paths.
- One sample per row unless `--runs N` says otherwise; the spread is reported where it was run.
- Anchors are capped at 24; Databricks's and PROGME's long lists are cut and the rows say so.
- Claim 1 and its dependents only.
- The comparison judge is a model; the floor and the control are its calibration, and a judge
  that cannot tell the measure from either has said so in the row.
